The contract as a whole

Identical in all three styles. Derived from the signature, domain, declarations, preconditions, powers and access-control policy.

Summary

VaccineProcurementC is an agreement between a Manufacturer (pfizer) and a Government (mcdc) for repeated orders of vaccine doses. The Government requests doses, the Manufacturer delivers them through a courier under cold-chain monitoring, and the Government pays within 30 days of each invoice. Four third parties take part without being bound by it: a Regulator, an Admin, the FDA and a WorldCourier.

Fixed when the contract is created

  • unit price of a dose (unitPrice)
  • smallest order accepted (minQuantity)
  • total doses available (maxQuantity, starting value of remain)
  • whether the vaccine is FDA-approved (approval)

Comes into force only if

the vaccine is FDA-approved (vaccineDose.FDAapproval == true).

Time is counted in

days.

What the parties owe

  • 4 obligations, all owed within the contract
  • 2 obligations that survive termination: payment (oPay) and recording an FDA withdrawal (oWithdrewApproval)
  • 2 powers, both able to terminate the contract

How it can end

  • The Manufacturer may terminate once the programme is complete (pTermination).
  • The Government may terminate after any stop-work order (pStopWork).

Who controls access to information

The Regulator administers the access policy and the delivery obligation. Nine rules: the Government may read the dose asset and the delivery record; the courier may read the FDA-approval flag; the Manufacturer may read FDA withdrawals; the Manufacturer may not write any of the five sensor values.

Monitoring

Five sensor feeds raised by the Regulator, controlled by the Manufacturer: temperature (value above −80, 5 readings within 10), humidity (above 70, 3 within 15), shock (above 5), light exposure and seal opening (any). Any alert for a request blocks that request's delivery from counting as fulfilled.

Normal course of one order

  1. Government requests doses (requested), creating the request, delivery, accounting and payment obligations for that request.
  2. Manufacturer informs the lead time; Government agrees (oAgreedOnRequest).
  3. Manufacturer notifies a delivery date; Government confirms the shipping location.
  4. Courier delivers on the notified (or risk-extended) date, with no cold-chain alert (oDeliver).
  5. Remaining doses and the amount payable are updated (oAssign).
  6. Invoice is issued; Government pays within 30 days (oPay, survives termination).
  7. When fewer than minQuantity doses remain and both parties declare termination, the programme is complete and the Manufacturer may terminate.

Observations

admin and fda are declared parties but appear in no obligation or power; fda performs the withdrewApproval event and grants Rule 9. adminStopWork, regulationStopWork and judicialStopWork are all performed by the Regulator. No postconditions or constraints are specified.

From the moment the contract takes effect, the Government owes the Manufacturer the completion of the procurement programme. The obligation counts as fulfilled only when fewer than the minimum order quantity of doses remain, the request, delivery and accounting obligations have all been fulfilled, and both parties have declared the agreement terminated. No deadline is set.

Fulfilling it is what unlocks the Manufacturer's power to terminate the contract.

Fromcontract start, unconditionally, Thenthe Government (mcdc) must, for the Manufacturer (pfizer), ensure that
  • fewer than minQuantity doses remain, and
  • oAgreedOnRequest, oDeliver and oAssign have been fulfilled, and

 Otherwisethe obligation is violated. No deadline. Enables pTermination. No access rules apply.

Each time the Government submits a request, this obligation is created, and it becomes binding once the Government signals its agreement. Each request also creates a delivery obligation on the Manufacturer towards the Government. It becomes binding once the request has been agreed and the Government has confirmed where to ship. The Manufacturer must then have announced a delivery date before that confirmation, and the courier must deliver FDA-approved doses to the confirmed location on the announced date, or on the later date the Manufacturer declared if an outside risk arose for this request. Delivery does not count if any cold-chain alert (temperature, seal opening, humidity, shock or light exposure) was raised for the request. All of these events must concern the same request.

The Regulator administers access to this obligation. The Government can read the dose asset and the delivery record; the courier can read the FDA flag; the Manufacturer cannot alter sensor readings (Rules 1–8). Fulfilling it triggers the accounting and payment obligations.

Ifthe Government submits a request (requested), WhenoAgreedOnRequest is fulfilled and the Government confirms the shipping location (confirmed), Thenthe Manufacturer must, for the Government, ensure that
  • the delivery-date notice (notifiedOD) came strictly before the confirmation,
  • the courier delivers (delivered) to the confirmed location,
  • vaccineDose.FDAapproval is true,
  • delivery date = notified date, or an outsideRisk was declared for this request and delivery date = its extended date,
  • no temperature, sealOpen, humidity, shock or lightExposure alert exists for this request,
  • all events share the request's reqID.
 Otherwisethe obligation is violated. Access administered by regulator. Rules 1–8 apply. Needs oAgreedOnRequest; drives oAssign, oPay, oRequestVaccineDosage.

Author's note: a delivery obligation is achieved if the delivering operation satisfies all the conditions required in a particular order.

Once doses for a request have been delivered, the Government is responsible for the bookkeeping: when the delivery obligation is fulfilled, the stock of remaining doses is reduced by the quantity delivered and the amount payable becomes that quantity multiplied by the unit price.

The amount set here is exactly what the payment obligation later requires.

Ifthe Government submits a request, Whendoses are delivered for that request, Thenthe Government must, for the Manufacturer, ensure that on fulfilment of oDeliver: remain.value −= delivered.dosage; paidAmount.value := delivered.dosage × vaccineDose.price. Otherwisethe obligation is violated. Needs oDeliver; feeds oPay, oRequestVaccineDosage. Rules 1, 3 apply.

If the FDA withdraws its approval, the Manufacturer must record that the vaccine is no longer FDA-approved. Because delivery and payment both require approval, this halts further deliveries and payments. The obligation survives the end of the contract.

The FDA grants the Manufacturer the right to see the withdrawal (Rule 9).
